The dataset now contains only the columns line, station, name, station
latitude / longitude, routes 1-9, entry, vending, entrance type, and ADA
compliance. We would still want to combine all the routes into one
column “routes served.” Line, Station Name, Entrance Type, and Vending
are all character variables. Entry and Ada compliance are logical
variables. Station Lat/Long is a numerical variable. Routes 1-11 are a
mix of number and character variables. This data is not fully tidy yet
especially the routes 1-9 variables these should be combined and be
character or numerical variables. The dimensions are 1868 X 19 with 19
columns and 1868 obs.

I imported the baseline data using `skip = 1` to drop the description
row and `na = "."` to code missing values. I then recoded sex and APOE4
carrier status so the values are labeled (“female”/“male” and
“carrier”/“non-carrier”) instead of 0 and 1. Participants who already
had MCI at baseline were removed, leaving 479 eligible participants. Of
these, 93 developed MCI during follow-up. The average baseline age was
65.03 years, and 30% of women in the study were APOE4 carriers.

8 participants appear only in the baseline dataset and 12 appear only in
the amyloid dataset. The files do not say why, but participants missing
from the amyloid data may have been enrolled without a biomarker
measurement or withdrawn early, while those missing from the baseline
data may lack intake records or come from a different recruitment
source, so they have no demographic or APOE4 information. I used an
inner join on `id` to keep only the 475 participants present in both
files, which gives one dataset linking baseline characteristics (age,
sex, education, APOE4 status, age at MCI onset) to amyloid ratios
measured at 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 years after baseline. The combined dataset
has 2375 rows, one per participant per visit, and was exported as
`mci_combined.csv`. It is not balanced: 169 amyloid values are still
missing because some participants skipped follow-up visits, so this will
need to be accounted for in later longitudinal analysis.
